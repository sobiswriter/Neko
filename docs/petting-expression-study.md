# Petting expression and gesture study — 2026-10-22

The approved revision needs subtle feline acting at a 62-pixel head width, with distinct physical stories. The earlier short clips and repeated upward eye arches made the actions difficult to distinguish.

## Reference and interpretation

[Cats Protection's body-language guidance](https://www.cats.org.uk/help-and-advice/cat-behaviour/cat-body-language) associates relaxed cats with soft/partly closed eyes, naturally forward relaxed ears, loose posture and slow blinking. Its guidance describes cheek rubbing and relaxed rolling too. [RSPCA's guide](https://nextgen.rspca.org.uk/en/animal-care/cat-body-language) similarly emphasizes reading eyes together with posture and tail. These references inform the acting; the wink, excited eye squeeze and deadpan yawn aftermath are stylized animation choices rather than literal feline facial-language claims.

## Translation into this character

- Keep the nose/eye spacing and head volume stable. Soft lids partly cover the existing eye position; they do not create a new face or raise giant human eyebrows.
- Let eyes/ears anticipate a head turn by a drawing. Let shoulders follow the head, with the tail arriving slightly later on a larger body movement.
- Relaxation: partial upper lids, a small quiet mouth, softly forward ears and weight released into the supporting paws. Use a slow reopen after contact, rather than an instant switch to the round-eyed idle.
- Wink: contact-side eye closes while the other remains softly open; include halfway closure and reopening. Keep the gesture brief and embedded in the offered paw or cheek press.
- Excitement: a short inward eye squeeze at the strongest reach or hop apex, then attentive open eyes on contact. Ears/body remain playful; avoid angry brows or defensive flattened ears.
- Sleepiness: eyes close before the little breathing beat; the one-eye peek precedes waking. Yawn uses real jaw opening/closing, then level half-lids and a closed mouth for the requested `=_=` expression.
- Avoid adding simultaneous ear, paw, tail and head wiggles to every hold. Put emphasis on one gesture; use restrained breathing/pressure changes where the action naturally continues.

## Visual checks

Check each face at native size on light/dark backgrounds, paired with its matching posture. Also inspect slow enlarged playback for eyes appearing/disappearing, mouth drift and head-volume changes. A correct face in a source sheet is insufficient if the actual held frame is too brief or the runtime calibration makes the feature disappear.

The first roll and kneading drafts exposed uneven source layout and recovery contamination. These drafts are excluded or re-extracted only where complete, clean drawings exist; failed recovery passages are redrawn through built-in ImageGen. Cropping/packing/rendering tools do not paint new expressions or anatomy.
